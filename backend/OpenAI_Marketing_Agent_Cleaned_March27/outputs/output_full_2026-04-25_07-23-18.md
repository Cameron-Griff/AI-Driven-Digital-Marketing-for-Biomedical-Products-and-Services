# 1) Detailed Marketing Plan

## Objective
Drive adoption of an AI-powered triage experience that helps patients and caregivers choose the right care setting—emergency, clinic, telehealth, or home care—while reinforcing that the tool is safety-first, explainable, and not diagnostic.

## Assumptions
- The product is already clinically reviewed for intended use and deployed with appropriate oversight.
- The solution is integrated into a patient-facing app and/or clinic/telehealth workflow.
- Privacy, consent, and data-handling practices are compliant with HIPAA and PIPEDA.
- The primary commercial motion includes both patient adoption and B2B2C adoption through clinics, telehealth providers, and health systems.
- The brand can publish educational content without making diagnostic claims.

## Positioning
**Core promise:** “Get clear, safe guidance on where to go next—fast.”
**Differentiators:**
- Explainable recommendations, not black-box advice
- Safety-first escalation to emergency care when needed
- 24/7 availability for initial guidance
- Helps reduce unnecessary ER visits and administrative burden
- Supports clinicians rather than replacing them

**Positioning statement:**  
For patients and care teams who need quick, trustworthy guidance during health uncertainty, this AI triage solution provides explainable, safety-first care navigation that recommends emergency, clinic, telehealth, or home care—helping improve access, reduce anxiety, and ease congestion without diagnosing or replacing clinicians.

## Audience Strategy
### Primary audiences
1. **Patients and caregivers**
   - Need reassurance, speed, and clarity
   - Often unsure whether symptoms are urgent
   - Want a trustworthy first step before choosing care

2. **Clinic staff and telehealth providers**
   - Need to manage demand and route patients appropriately
   - Want fewer avoidable emergency escalations
   - Need tools that fit existing workflows

3. **Healthcare administrators**
   - Focused on patient flow, resource utilization, and operational efficiency
   - Want measurable impact on wait times and administrative burden

## Messaging Pillars
### 1. Safety-first guidance
- Emphasize that recommendations prioritize safety and escalation when appropriate.
- Avoid overpromising; the tool supports decision-making, it does not diagnose.

### 2. Explainability and trust
- Show users why a recommendation was made in plain language.
- Provide clear reasoning, symptom prompts, and urgent warning signs.

### 3. Access and convenience
- 24/7 availability for initial triage.
- Reduce the friction of figuring out where to go.

### 4. System efficiency
- Help redirect low-acuity cases away from emergency departments when appropriate.
- Support staff by reducing avoidable inbound questions and intake burden.

### 5. Privacy and compliance
- Make privacy handling visible and understandable.
- Reassure users that sensitive data is handled responsibly.

# 2) Platform-Specific Playbooks

## Patient-facing app playbook
### Goal
Maximize trust and completion while helping users reach the right level of care quickly.

### Best practices
- Place triage access prominently on home screen and symptom-related entry points.
- Use calm, plain-language prompts.
- Show progress indicators so users know the process is short and guided.
- Present urgency results with strong visual hierarchy:
  - Emergency
  - Same-day clinic/telehealth
  - Routine care
  - Home care / self-care guidance
- Include an immediate escalation banner for red-flag symptoms.

### Content format
- Structured symptom checker
- Short explanatory text after each question
- “Why am I seeing this?” tooltips
- Safety disclaimer before and after completion
- Privacy reminder near data entry

### CTA examples
- “Start symptom check”
- “Get care guidance now”
- “See what level of care is appropriate”

### Tone
- Reassuring
- Non-alarmist
- Clear and direct
- Never suggest certainty where there is uncertainty

## Clinic integration playbook
### Goal
Reduce front-desk burden and streamline routing into the right care pathway.

### Best practices
- Embed triage into patient intake or pre-visit workflows.
- Send summarized results to staff in a concise, readable format.
- Flag cases needing urgent human review.
- Allow clinic-specific routing rules aligned to care pathways.

### Messaging for clinic staff
- Fewer unnecessary calls and intake bottlenecks
- Better patient navigation before arrival
- More time for high-need patients
- Supports, rather than disrupts, existing workflows

### Content format
- Workflow diagram
- “What the triage result means” summary
- Staff guidance page
- Integration overview
- Clinical oversight and escalation policy

### CTA examples
- “See how triage fits your workflow”
- “Request a clinic demo”
- “Review integration options”

## Telehealth provider playbook
### Goal
Improve visit appropriateness and direct patients to the right channel faster.

### Best practices
- Use triage as a pre-visit step to route care efficiently.
- Highlight when telehealth is appropriate vs when in-person escalation is needed.
- Provide clinicians with a concise symptom summary and urgency context.
- Reinforce that the tool supports provider judgment.

### Content format
- Provider landing page
- Pre-visit routing explainer
- FAQ on clinical oversight
- Safety escalation framework
- Operational efficiency brief

### CTA examples
- “Optimize patient routing”
- “Reduce avoidable escalation”
- “See telehealth workflow demo”

## Healthcare administrator playbook
### Goal
Build the business case around capacity, efficiency, and patient experience.

### Best practices
- Lead with operational outcomes and risk-managed deployment.
- Show how the tool can help reduce overcrowding and administrative burden.
- Include privacy, security, and compliance assurances early.
- Use measurable pilot goals and a phased rollout.

### Content format
- ROI one-pager
- Executive summary deck
- Pilot proposal
- Security and compliance overview
- Case study framework

### CTA examples
- “Schedule an executive briefing”
- “Review pilot objectives”
- “Discuss implementation”

## Compliance and trust playbook
### Must-have elements everywhere
- Clear “not a diagnosis” language
- Emergency escalation messaging
- Data privacy and consent notice
- Security reassurance without overclaiming
- Clinical oversight statement

### Recommended trust cues
- “Explainable recommendations”
- “Reviewed for safety”
- “Designed to support clinicians”
- “Your data is handled with care”
