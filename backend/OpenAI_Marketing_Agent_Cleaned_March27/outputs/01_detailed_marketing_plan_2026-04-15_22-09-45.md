# 1) Detailed Marketing Plan

## Assumptions
- Primary market is **Canada-first**, with **English-language** assets initially.
- The product is **not a diagnostic tool**; all marketing must emphasize **triage, guidance, escalation, and safety**.
- Buyer groups include both **B2C users** (patients/caregivers) and **B2B buyers** (clinics, telehealth providers, hospitals, administrators).
- The strongest credibility angles are **Ottawa/Ontario-based trust signals**, **privacy/compliance**, and **safety-first explainability**.
- The platform already has or is developing integrations with clinic/telehealth workflows, but the marketing plan should work even if integrations are still limited.

## Positioning
**Primary positioning statement:**  
An AI-powered digital triage platform that helps patients get to the right care level faster—emergency, clinic, or home care—while supporting safer flow for healthcare organizations.

**Core message pillars:**
1. **Safety-first guidance**  
   Clear escalation for urgent symptoms and conservative, non-diagnostic recommendations.
2. **Less uncertainty for patients**  
   Helps people know what to do next without overwhelming them.
3. **Improved care flow for providers**  
   Reduces unnecessary ER visits and routes patients more efficiently.
4. **Trust and compliance**  
   Privacy-conscious, Canada-first, and designed with healthcare realities in mind.
5. **Explainable AI**  
   Recommendations are understandable, not a black box.

## Target Audiences and Messaging

### Patients and caregivers
**Needs:** quick clarity, reassurance, safe next steps  
**Message:** “Unsure what care you need? Get guided to the right level of care quickly and safely.”  
**CTA:** “Check symptoms” / “Find next step”

### Clinics and telehealth providers
**Needs:** reduce intake burden, improve routing, support staff efficiency  
**Message:** “Help patients self-navigate before they arrive, so your team can focus on the right cases.”  
**CTA:** “See how triage integrates”

### Hospitals and administrators
**Needs:** flow management, operational efficiency, trust, compliance  
**Message:** “Improve patient routing and reduce avoidable congestion with safety-first digital triage.”  
**CTA:** “Book a workflow review”

### Healthcare systems / network leaders
**Needs:** scalability, governance, measurable outcomes  
**Message:** “A triage layer that supports better access, better flow, and better patient experience.”  
**CTA:** “Request a demo”

## Funnel Strategy

### Awareness
Goal: establish credibility and explain what the platform does without overclaiming.
- Educational content about common care-navigation problems
- Search-focused pages for symptom navigation and care-level guidance
- Trust-building content on privacy, safety, and limitations of AI
- Thought leadership on ER overcrowding, access, and patient routing in Canada

### Consideration
Goal: show how the product works and why it is safer/more useful than generic symptom checkers.
- Product explainer pages
- Side-by-side “what it does / what it does not do”
- Integration and implementation content for clinics and telehealth teams
- Case-study-style outcomes framing, if available

### Conversion
Goal: move users to action.
- Patients: symptom check entry point, prominent “urgent symptoms” escalation
- B2B: demo request, contact sales, workflow assessment
- Providers: integration consultation, pilot request, implementation checklist

### Retention / Expansion
Goal: maintain trust and drive repeat usage or organizational expansion.
- Follow-up educational content
- Seasonal symptom routing content
- Healthcare organization onboarding resources
- Product updates focused on safety, explainability, and workflow improvements

## Content Strategy

### Content themes
1. **Know what to do next**
   - “When should I go to ER vs clinic vs home care?”
   - “What to do if symptoms are concerning”
2. **Safety and trust**
   - AI limitations
   - Privacy and data handling
   - Why escalation matters
3. **Care flow and access**
   - Reducing avoidable ER visits
   - Supporting intake teams
   - Better routing for telehealth
4. **Canadian healthcare context**
   - Access challenges
   - Ontario/Ottawa credibility
   - Practical navigation for Canadian patients
5. **Explainable AI in healthcare**
   - Why transparent recommendations matter
   - How conservative guidance supports safety

### Content formats
- SEO landing pages
- Blog articles
- FAQ and help-center articles
- Short explainer videos
- Provider-facing one-pagers
- Case studies and pilot summaries
- Webinars for administrators and clinical partners
- In-app educational prompts and microcopy

## Channel Strategy

### Website
Primary conversion hub for both patients and B2B prospects.
- Create distinct paths: “For Patients” and “For Clinics & Providers”
- Build trust pages: privacy, safety, compliance, clinical disclaimers
- Add clear emergency escalation language above the fold
- Use Canada-specific proof points and contact options

### Patient app
Use as a retention and engagement channel.
- Guided triage entry
- Contextual reassurance and next-step education
- Post-triage resources
- Push notifications for health guidance only where appropriate and compliant

### Clinic/telehealth integrations
Use as a B2B enablement channel.
- Integration pages
- Partner onboarding materials
- Workflow fit content
- Implementation support assets

## KPIs
### Patient-facing KPIs
- Symptom intake completion rate
- Triage-to-action rate
- ER escalation compliance for high-risk symptoms
- Return usage rate
- Time to recommendation

### B2B KPIs
- Demo requests
- Qualified leads
- Pilot starts
- Integration discussions
- Conversion from pilot to contract
- Time saved in intake workflows, if measurable

## Risk Controls in Marketing
- Never imply diagnosis
- Always include emergency escalation language
- Avoid claims of replacing clinicians
- Use plain-language disclaimers
- Ensure privacy language is easy to find and understand
