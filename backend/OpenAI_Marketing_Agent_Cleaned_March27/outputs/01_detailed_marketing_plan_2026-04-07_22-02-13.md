# 1) Detailed Marketing Plan

Executive summary
- Objective: Drive adoption of an AI-powered, explainable triage product to reduce non-urgent ED visits and improve patient routing, access, and healthcare efficiency in the US and Canada.
- Primary audiences: worried patients/caregivers (consumer users), clinic intake teams and telehealth providers (operational users), ED managers and health system administrators (buyers/partners).
- Core positioning: “Safety-first, explainable triage that helps you get the right care — fast.” Emphasize that the product is NOT diagnostic; it triages, escalates emergencies, and provides actionable next steps with referrals and clinic integration.

Assumptions (explicit)
- Launch timeline: 12-week go-to-market (GTM) from plan start to public launch.
- Budget: modest growth budget (mix of paid search/social + partnership outreach). If budget is higher/lower, channel emphasis should adjust.
- Team: small cross-functional GTM team (1 Growth Lead, 1 Product Marketer, 1 Partnerships Lead, 1 Content/SEO, 1 Clinical Lead, 2 Engineers for integrations).
- Existing assets: product in mature beta with working integrations/APIs; brand identity and app UI largely complete.
- Legal/compliance: HIPAA/PIPEDA compliance framework, privacy policy and legal disclaimers ready but require final signoff for specific partner integrations.
- Languages: English primary; French Canadian localization available for patient-facing content.
- Tracking & analytics: basic analytics & attribution are available (GA4, Mixpanel, MMP for app installs). CRM in place for B2B outreach.

Positioning & key messaging
- Primary value message: “Explainable triage that guides people to the right level of care — quickly, safely, and 24/7.”
- Supporting messages:
  - Safety-first: clinician-reviewed escalation logic; clear emergency prompts.
  - Explainability: short, plain-language explanations of why a level-of-care recommendation was made.
  - System benefit: reduces ED overcrowding and improves clinic throughput.
  - Trust & privacy: HIPAA and PIPEDA compliant; data used only for care routing with clear consent.
- Tone & style: reassuring, clinical, concise, accessible, non-alarming.

Targeting & go-to-market motions
- Consumer acquisition: SEM (symptom + “should I go to ER” intent), organic SEO (symptom pages + explainers), app-store presence, targeted social ads to demographics with high ED utilization, referral partnerships with clinics and telehealth providers.
- B2B partnerships: outreach to ED managers, clinic networks, telehealth providers, and payers for pilot integrations and co-branded rollouts.
- Channel mix (initial): 40% organic/SEO + content, 30% partnerships & pilots, 20% SEM & app-store, 10% paid social & PR.
- Launch priority: secure 2–4 pilot clinical partners (community clinics and one health system ED) before wide consumer push to validate downstream referral & ROI.

Growth levers & conversion funnel
- Acquire: symptom intent ads, SEO symptom triage pages, referral links from clinics/telehealth, app store optimization.
- Activate: frictionless interactive triage wizard; require minimal registration to get recommendation, optional account for history/referrals.
- Retain/Repeat: send referral follow-up, care reminders, clinic booking links, post-triage satisfaction survey and NPS.
- Expand: B2B integrations and contracts, monetization via per-referral engagement fee or subscription for clinics/health systems.

Core KPIs
- Product usage: triage completion rate, average time to completion, escalation accuracy (clinician review).
- User outcomes: % of users redirected from ED to appropriate care (measured in pilot), referral click-through rate, booking completion rate.
- Business: pilot conversions (number of clinics integrated), CAC (consumer vs. B2B), demo-to-deal conversion for health systems, reduction in non-urgent ED visits in pilot sites.
- Engagement & satisfaction: NPS, CSAT, retention at 7/30 days.

Comms & content strategy
- Content pillars: symptom explainers (patient-centered), safety & how-it-works explainers (trust-building), clinic/partner case studies (B2B), FAQ on privacy & limitations.
- Formats: interactive symptom wizard, short explainers (300–700 words), quick videos (30–60s) for clinic staff and consumers, email flows (post-triage), one-pagers & slide decks for partners.
- Localization: English + French Canadian consumer content; clinical/B2B collateral in English (translate on demand).

Compliance, legal & risk mitigation
- Prominent non-diagnostic disclaimers and emergency escalation language at first step.
- Explicit consent before collecting PHI; granular controls for data sharing with clinic partners.
- Clinical governance: editorial clinical lead reviews messaging and triage explainers; periodic audit of triage outcomes vs. clinician review.
- Liability mapping: clear contracts with partners specifying triage is advisory; recommend liability review for pilots.

Budget & resource recommendations (high-level)
- Initial 12-week runway: allocate developer/time for integrations + marketing spend for SEM and paid social tests, travel/compensation for pilot clinics.
- Contingency: set aside ~15% for legal/translation/clinical validation iterations.

Risks & mitigations
- Risk: public mistrust of automated triage. Mitigation: heavy emphasis on explainability, clinician oversight, testimonials and pilot results.
- Risk: regulatory/legal pushback. Mitigation: early legal engagement for pilot sites; conservative disclaimers.
- Risk: low referral completion from triage to booking. Mitigation: frictionless booking links, co-developed clinic UX, follow-up nudges.

Launch plan summary (high-level timeline)
- Weeks 1–4: finalize messaging, legal signoffs, build landing page, start partnership outreach.
- Weeks 5–8: run small pilots, iterate UX/flows, publish SEO content and app store assets.
- Weeks 9–12: public launch, paid acquisition ramp, scale partnerships and PR.
