# 1) Detailed Marketing Plan

## Objectives
- Reduce unnecessary emergency department visits by guiding users to the right care setting faster.
- Build trust in an AI-assisted, safety-first triage experience.
- Drive adoption across three entry points: direct-to-patient app, clinic integration, and telehealth integration.
- Position the product as an explainable care-navigation layer, not a diagnostic tool.

## Assumptions
- The product is early-stage and available first in English, with localization planned by market.
- Clinical oversight is in place for content governance, escalation rules, and safety review.
- Regulatory and privacy requirements are handled through implementation controls and legal review.
- Conversion success is measured by completed triage sessions, recommended care-path adherence, and partner activations rather than diagnosis outcomes.

## Positioning
**Core position:** A safe, explainable triage companion that helps patients decide where to go next, 24/7.

**Message pillars**
1. **Safety first**  
   Clear escalation prompts for emergency symptoms and guidance that errs on the side of caution.
2. **Explainable guidance**  
   Users see why a recommendation was made in plain language.
3. **Faster care navigation**  
   Helps reduce uncertainty, avoid delays, and match care setting to need.
4. **Operational relief**  
   Helps clinics, telehealth providers, and health systems reduce avoidable ED pressure.

**Messaging boundaries**
- Do not claim diagnosis.
- Do not imply replacing clinicians.
- Do not position as personalized medical advice beyond triage and navigation.
- Always include emergency disclaimers and privacy language where relevant.

## Target segments and value propositions
### 1. Patients
- Need: “What should I do right now?”
- Value: Immediate, reassuring, step-by-step triage with clear next action.
- CTA: Start symptom check.

### 2. Caregivers
- Need: Help deciding for a child, elder, or dependent.
- Value: Structured guidance that reduces fear of missing an emergency.
- CTA: Check symptoms for someone else.

### 3. Clinic staff
- Need: Reduce front-desk burden and improve routing.
- Value: A consistent triage pre-check that helps direct patients to the right service.
- CTA: Integrate triage into intake workflows.

### 4. Telehealth providers
- Need: Route low-acuity users efficiently and identify escalation cases.
- Value: Better visit prioritization and safer intake handoff.
- CTA: Add triage before appointment booking.

### 5. Healthcare system administrators
- Need: Reduce ED overcrowding and improve flow.
- Value: A measurable navigation layer with governance and compliance controls.
- CTA: Pilot system integration.

## Funnel strategy
### Awareness
- Educational content about when to seek urgent care vs. routine care.
- Trust-building materials on safety, privacy, and explainability.
- PR and thought leadership around ED overcrowding and access to care.

### Consideration
- Interactive demos showing triage workflow and escalation logic.
- Case examples for common symptoms and care settings.
- Partner-specific landing pages for clinics and telehealth organizations.

### Conversion
- In-app onboarding with immediate “check symptoms now” CTA.
- Seamless integration prompts for clinic and telehealth partners.
- Short, confidence-building copy emphasizing safety and clarity.

### Retention
- Follow-up prompts after triage completion.
- Saved preferences for future use.
- Partner analytics dashboards highlighting routing outcomes and usage.

## Content strategy
### Core content themes
- “What to do next” decision support.
- Safe escalation and emergency awareness.
- Care navigation by symptom severity.
- How the triage workflow works and what it does not do.
- Privacy, compliance, and explainability.

### Content formats
- Interactive symptom checker
- Short explainer videos
- FAQ pages
- Partner integration one-pagers
- Clinic intake email templates
- Trust and safety pages
- Scenario-based educational cards

## Channel strategy
### Direct-to-patient app
- Primary acquisition and engagement channel.
- Use app store assets, onboarding flows, push notifications, and in-app education.
- Emphasize immediate usefulness and reassurance.

### Clinic integrations
- Embed triage into appointment scheduling, intake, and patient portal workflows.
- Use staff enablement materials, implementation guides, and patient-facing handouts.
- Focus on operational efficiency and better routing.

### Telehealth integrations
- Add triage before visit booking or during intake.
- Use provider-facing summaries and escalation flags.
- Focus on match quality, faster escalation, and lower inappropriate visits.

## Trust, safety, and compliance marketing
- Prominently disclose that the tool does not diagnose.
- Include emergency call instructions for life-threatening symptoms.
- Present privacy safeguards in plain language.
- Use medically reviewed content labels where applicable.
- Maintain a clinical review process for all symptom content updates.

## KPIs
### Patient-facing
- Symptom-check completion rate
- CTA click-through to recommended care setting
- Return usage rate
- Escalation adherence rate

### Partner-facing
- Number of pilots launched
- Integration completion rate
- Reduction in avoidable ED referrals
- Provider satisfaction and workflow adoption

### Trust metrics
- Content comprehension rate
- Support tickets related to safety/confusion
- Complaint rate regarding recommendation clarity

## Launch phases
### Phase 1: Trust foundation
- Publish safety, privacy, and explainability pages.
- Launch a limited symptom set with high-confidence triage paths.
- Train support and clinical governance teams.

### Phase 2: Patient growth
- Release app onboarding and educational campaign.
- Launch symptom-specific landing pages.
- Start paid and organic traffic to high-intent queries.

### Phase 3: Partner expansion
- Deploy clinic and telehealth integrations.
- Create partner case studies and operational proof points.
- Add region-specific localization and compliance adaptation.

# 2) Platform-Specific Playbooks

## Direct-to-patient app playbook
**Goal:** Drive first-time use and repeat engagement.

**Primary message**
- “Not sure what care you need? Get clear, safety-first guidance in minutes.”

**Best content**
- App store screenshots showing the triage flow
- Short onboarding video
- Symptom-specific entry points
- Push notifications for saved follow-ups
- Trust page with privacy and emergency disclaimers

**Creative guidance**
- Use calm colors, simple icons, and large readable text.
- Show the user journey: symptom → questions → explanation → recommended next step.
- Avoid overly “AI” language; emphasize safety and clarity.

**CTA examples**
- Start symptom check
- Check symptoms now
- See recommended care options

**Conversion tips**
- Keep the first screen frictionless.
- Make emergency escalation visible early.
- Show “why this recommendation” immediately after triage.
- Offer a clear next-action button to urgent care, primary care, telehealth, or emergency services depending on result.

## Clinic integration playbook
**Goal:** Reduce front-desk burden and improve patient routing.

**Primary message**
- “Help patients arrive at the right level of care before they call, book, or walk in.”

**Best content**
- Front-desk workflow sheet
- Patient intake QR code poster
- Staff training deck
- Compliance and oversight summary
- Implementation one-pager for administrators

**Placement ideas**
- Pre-visit patient portal
- QR code on reception signage
- SMS link in appointment reminders
- Embedded intake step for new patients

**Operational considerations**
- Clarify that staff can review outputs but should not treat the tool as diagnostic.
- Add escalation protocols for concerning symptom patterns.
- Provide a handoff summary that is easy to scan and document.

**CTA examples**
- Add triage to intake
- Request a pilot
- See clinic workflow demo

## Telehealth integration playbook
**Goal:** Improve booking quality and prioritize urgent cases.

**Primary message**
- “Route patients to the right virtual or in-person care faster, with safety-first triage.”

**Best content**
- Provider intake summary example
- Booking flow mockup
- Escalation workflow diagram
- Partner ROI snapshot
- Safety and governance brief

**Placement ideas**
- Before scheduling
- During pre-visit symptom collection
- In post-visit follow-up recommendations
- On telehealth provider landing pages

**Operational considerations**
- Trigger immediate escalation when red-flag symptoms appear.
- Summarize triage context for clinicians in a concise format.
- Ensure the system supports oversight, auditability, and privacy controls.

**CTA examples**
- Add triage to booking
- View provider workflow
- Start a pilot

## Healthcare system administrator playbook
**Goal:** Establish strategic pilots and scale adoption.

**Primary message**
- “Reduce avoidable ED congestion while improving patient navigation and safety.”

**Best content**
- Executive brief
- ROI and capacity planning summary
- Safety/compliance overview
- Pilot success metrics
- Stakeholder map

**Selling points**
- Better routing to the right care setting
- Reduced unnecessary ED volume
- Standardized, explainable triage pathway
- Governance-ready deployment model

## Trust and safety playbook
**Goal:** Prevent misunderstanding and reinforce legitimacy.

**Must-have elements**
- Plain-language disclaimer: not a diagnosis tool
- Emergency warning banner
- Privacy and data-use summary
- Clinical oversight statement
- Region-specific compliance references where required

**Recommended copy pattern**
- “This tool helps guide care choices. It does not diagnose conditions. If you think this may be an emergency, call local emergency services now.”
