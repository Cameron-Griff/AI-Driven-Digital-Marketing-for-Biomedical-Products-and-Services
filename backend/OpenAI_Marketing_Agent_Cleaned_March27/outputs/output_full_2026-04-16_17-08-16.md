# 1) Detailed Marketing Plan

## Assumptions
- The brand is a Canadian boutique home furnishings and interior design business with a premium, service-led positioning.
- Primary revenue comes from a mix of product sales, interior design services, virtual design, and curated sourcing support.
- Website ecommerce and service landing pages are the main conversion surfaces, with social media and email used to drive traffic back to the site.
- The brand is not trying to compete on price or mass assortment; it wins through taste, curation, trust, and convenience.
- The audience is mainly in consideration and decision stages, so messaging should reduce overwhelm and increase confidence.

## Marketing objectives
1. Increase qualified traffic to product, lookbook, journal, and service pages.
2. Convert visitors into:
   - ecommerce purchases
   - interior design inquiries
   - virtual design bookings
   - curated sourcing requests
3. Build brand authority as a stylish, editorial, boutique design destination.
4. Increase average order value through bundled styling recommendations and founder picks.
5. Improve trust and reduce purchase hesitation with clear service explanations, portfolio proof, and Canadian shipping transparency.

## Positioning
**Core positioning statement:**  
A refined Canadian destination for curated furnishings and thoughtful interior design support, helping stylish homeowners create personal, cohesive spaces with expert guidance and exclusive founder-selected pieces.

## Primary audience segments
- **Homeowners refreshing a room:** Want curated pieces and styling direction without hiring a full designer.
- **Renovators/new-build clients:** Need cohesive decisions across finishes, furniture, and decor.
- **Design-conscious shoppers:** Appreciate unique, boutique selections and editorial presentation.
- **Clients seeking support:** Want a designer’s eye through in-person or virtual services.

## Messaging pillars
### 1. Curated with confidence
Emphasize handpicked pieces, founder favorites, and a smaller, more intentional selection that saves customers time.

### 2. Design support, not just products
Position services as a differentiator: product recommendations, room planning, virtual design, and sourcing are part of the experience.

### 3. Boutique, personal, polished
Use warm editorial language to reinforce an elevated but approachable brand feel.

### 4. Stylish homes made simpler
Reduce overwhelm by showing how one source can help with style cohesion and decision-making.

### 5. Canadian-friendly service
Highlight local relevance, shipping considerations, and privacy-conscious consultations.

## Funnel strategy
### Awareness
Goal: Inspire and educate.
- Lookbook/editorial content
- Journal posts on styling trends, room makeovers, and design tips
- Social snippets that drive to the website
- SEO for style, room, and service keywords

### Consideration
Goal: Build confidence and shortlist the brand.
- Product pages with room context, dimensions, and styling suggestions
- Service pages with clear process, deliverables, timelines, and FAQs
- Portfolio/case studies
- Testimonials and founder story

### Decision
Goal: Remove friction and prompt action.
- Strong CTAs: “Book a Consultation,” “Request a Design Proposal,” “Shop the Look”
- Clear service packages or starting prices if available
- Shipping/returns clarity
- Contact form with low-friction prompts
- Abandoned cart and inquiry follow-up emails

## Content strategy
### Website content
- **Homepage:** Hero-led inspiration, curated value proposition, service CTA, featured collections, founder picks, testimonials.
- **Product listings:** Use editorial naming, room use cases, styling notes, dimensions, material details, and related items.
- **Lookbook:** Seasonal room stories, shoppable vignettes, “shop the look” modules.
- **Journal:** Educational and inspirational posts targeting search intent.
- **Portfolio:** Before/after projects, design process, client goals, results, and product sourcing highlights.
- **Service pages:** Separate landing pages for interior design, virtual design, and sourcing support.

### Recommended content themes
- Room-by-room styling guidance
- How to choose cohesive furnishings
- Canadian home design tips for new builds/renovations
- Founder picks and why they matter
- Virtual design process explained
- Seasonal refresh ideas for living rooms, bedrooms, dining spaces, and entryways
- Material, texture, and scale education
- “How we work” transparency content

## SEO plan
Target high-intent and mid-funnel keywords such as:
- interior design services Canada
- virtual interior design Canada
- curated home furnishings
- boutique furniture store Canada
- designer selected home decor
- living room styling ideas
- how to create a cohesive home
- room design consultation

SEO priorities:
- Build service landing pages around distinct intent
- Add structured internal linking from journal posts to service pages and relevant products
- Use descriptive alt text and image metadata
- Include Canadian shipping and service area language where relevant
- Optimize for mobile usability and page speed, while supporting desktop-first browsing behavior

## Conversion strategy
### On-site conversion elements
- Prominent CTA buttons on homepage and service pages
- “Shop the look” modules on lookbook and portfolio pages
- Lead capture form offering a design consultation or style guide
- Product recommendation blocks for cross-sell and upsell
- Trust signals: testimonials, process overview, credentials, press mentions if available

### Lead generation offers
- Complimentary discovery call
- Style questionnaire
- Virtual design consult inquiry form
- Downloadable guide: “How to Start a Cohesive Room”
- Seasonal inspiration email signup

## Email strategy
- **Welcome series:** Brand story, design philosophy, bestsellers, services overview
- **Browse abandonment:** Featured products, styling ideas, related pieces
- **Cart abandonment:** Reminder plus service-led reassurance
- **Inquiry follow-up:** Process explanation, FAQs, portfolio examples, next steps
- **Newsletter:** New arrivals, seasonal edits, founder picks, journal highlights

## Social media role
Social should support discovery, trust, and repeat website visits.
- Pinterest: shoppable inspiration and lookbook traffic
- Instagram: editorial imagery, reels, process content, founder voice
- Optional Facebook: local credibility and retargeting support
- Optional TikTok: short-form styling tips if brand resources allow

## Paid media strategy
Use paid media selectively and promotion-sensitively.
- Retarget website visitors with lookbook and service ads
- Promote best-selling or signature curated items
- Drive leads to service landing pages rather than generic homepage traffic
- Use seasonal campaigns rather than constant discounting

## KPIs
- Website conversion rate
- Inquiry form completion rate
- Consultation booking rate
- Average order value
- Product page engagement
- Email signup rate
- Return visitor rate
- Organic traffic to journal and service pages
- Assisted conversions from social and email

## Brand guardrails
- Avoid discount-heavy language that weakens premium positioning
- Keep visuals editorial and uncluttered
- Use warm, confident, design-savvy copy
- Ensure service messaging is clear, concise, and privacy-aware
- Include Canada-specific shipping and fulfillment expectations where needed

# 2) Platform-Specific Playbooks

## Website ecommerce playbook
### Goal
Turn inspiration into product discovery and purchases.

### Best practices
- Use large, high-quality images with multiple angles and room context.
- Add concise editorial copy explaining why each piece matters.
- Include dimensions, materials, care instructions, lead times, and shipping notes.
- Add “pair with” and “style with” suggestions to increase basket size.
- Feature founder picks and limited-availability items prominently.
- Make mobile checkout simple, but preserve a refined desktop browsing experience.

### Page types to prioritize
- Homepage
- Collection pages
- Product detail pages
- Lookbook/Shop the Look pages
- Cart and checkout
- FAQ/shipping/returns pages

### CTA examples
- Shop the Look
- See Founder Picks
- Add to Cart
- Request Sourcing Help
- Book a Design Consultation

## Interior design service landing page playbook
### Goal
Convert interested visitors into qualified leads.

### Best practices
- Spell out who the service is for.
- Explain the process step by step.
- Clarify deliverables, timelines, and what clients receive.
- Include proof: portfolio, testimonials, and before/after imagery.
- Reduce anxiety with FAQs around budget, process, and communication.
- Offer a short inquiry form to lower friction.

### Suggested sections
- Hero with clear value statement
- Who it’s for
- Service options
- Process
- Portfolio snapshots
- Testimonials
- FAQ
- Inquiry form

### CTA examples
- Book a Consultation
- Start Your Design Project
- Enquire About Virtual Design
- Tell Us About Your Space

## Virtual design playbook
### Goal
Capture out-of-region or convenience-driven clients.

### Best practices
- Emphasize flexibility, clear deliverables, and how virtual support works.
- Highlight that clients can access curated expertise from anywhere in Canada.
- Show sample deliverables: concept boards, sourcing lists, room plans, product links.
- Make communication expectations very clear.

### CTA examples
- See How Virtual Design Works
- Request a Virtual Consultation
- Get a Custom Room Plan

## Journal/content marketing playbook
### Goal
Build authority, inspire, and improve SEO.

### Content formats
- Styling guides
- Trend interpretations
- Behind-the-scenes sourcing stories
- Room transformations
- Founder’s notes on seasonal edits
- Design mistakes to avoid
- Canadian home styling tips

### Voice
Warm, polished, practical, and informed. The tone should feel like a trusted designer speaking to a style-conscious client.

### Internal linking strategy
- Link every journal post to relevant product pages
- Link educational posts to service landing pages
- Link portfolio stories to shoppable items where appropriate

## Portfolio playbook
### Goal
Prove expertise and make abstract services tangible.

### Best practices
- Structure each project around client challenge, design approach, result.
- Include before/after where possible.
- Highlight product sourcing, layout decisions, and material selection.
- Add “shop similar” modules to inspire immediate action.

### CTA examples
- Start a Similar Project
- Book a Design Inquiry
- Shop This Style

## Social media playbook
### Instagram
- Use for editorial visuals, reels, room reveals, founder commentary, and social proof.
- Content ratio suggestion: inspiration, educational snippets, process, product highlights, community/trust.

### Pinterest
- Treat as a visual search engine.
- Pin lookbooks, product collages, room ideas, and portfolio images with keyword-rich descriptions.

### Facebook
- Use for retargeting, local trust, and event/service promotion if relevant.

## Email playbook
### Segments
- New subscribers
- Browsers
- Cart abandoners
- Buyers
- Service leads
- Past clients

### Cadence
- Weekly or biweekly newsletter
- Triggered flows for welcome, browse abandonment, cart abandonment, and inquiry follow-up

### Content mix
- New arrivals
- Founder picks
- Design tips
- Seasonal edits
- Portfolio highlights
- Service reminders

## CRM/inquiry management playbook
- Respond quickly to inquiries with a polished, helpful tone.
- Use a short qualification form to capture room type, budget range, timeline, and design goals.
- Send a follow-up email with next steps and examples of relevant work.
- Track lead source and service interest for future marketing refinement.
