# 1) Detailed Marketing Plan

Summary / Positioning
- Product: AI-powered, safety-first triage that provides explainable, non-diagnostic care direction to reduce non-urgent ED visits and patient uncertainty.
- Core promise: Safer, clearer care routing — explainable recommendations and emergency escalation without replacing clinicians.
- Tone: Reassuring, authoritative, empathetic, concise, safety-focused and non-alarmist.
- Primary business goal: Reduce non-urgent ED visits and improve care navigation efficiency across healthcare settings (health systems, clinics, telehealth, consumer apps).

Target audiences (priority order)
1. Health system administrators (decision-makers who measure ED throughput, cost and patient safety).
2. Clinic operations managers / front-desk staff (workflow improvement, reduced inappropriate referrals).
3. Telehealth partners and clinicians (reliable triage to improve clinician efficiency and safety).
4. Symptom-concerned patients and caregivers (trustworthy guidance, privacy assurance).
5. Regulators / clinical advisors / payers (compliance and outcomes).

Key marketing objectives & KPIs
- Operational outcomes (primary): % reduction in non-urgent ED visits for pilot populations; reduction in average time-to-care routing; % of escalations that resulted in appropriate ED presentation.
- Adoption & engagement: Monthly active users within partner systems; triage completion rate; recommendation acceptance rate; clinician handoff open/read rate.
- Trust & satisfaction: NPS, patient satisfaction for triage interactions, clinician satisfaction.
- Commercial: Number of signed health system integrations; pilot-to-contract conversion rate; CAC and LTV estimates for enterprise sales.

Core messages (by audience)
- Health systems: "Reduce unnecessary ED burden with an explainable, privacy-first triage layer that routes patients safely and measurably."
- Clinics/Telehealth: "Decrease inappropriate ED referrals and streamline clinician handoffs with clear, evidence-aligned care direction."
- Patients/caregivers: "Get clear, empathetic guidance now — if you need emergency care, we escalate immediately. Your privacy is protected."
- Regulators/clinicians: "Non-diagnostic, rule-backed AI with emergency escalation and explainability; designed to complement clinical judgment."

Value props to emphasize
- Safety-first: built-in emergency escalation and clinician handoff.
- Explainability: transparent reasoning for each recommendation.
- Privacy & compliance: HIPAA/PIPEDA-first architecture; minimal PHI transfer unless necessary.
- Non-diagnostic stance: clear disclaimers and role as navigational aid only.
- Integration-ready: APIs/SDKs for portals, telehealth stacks, and mobile apps.

Go-to-market phases
1. Pilot & Validation (0–3 months)
   - Run 2–3 targeted pilots with health systems and telehealth partners.
   - Focus KPIs: reduction in non-urgent ED visits, triage completion, escalation accuracy.
   - Tactics: clinician champion recruitment, co-branded pilot dashboards, IRB/regulatory readouts where needed.
2. Expand & Proof (3–9 months)
   - Publish case studies, anonymized outcome data, and operational playbooks.
   - Tactics: targeted enterprise sales outreach, webinars, conference presentations, clinician testimonials.
3. Scale & Consumer Enablement (9–18 months)
   - Broaden consumer web/mobile distribution via telehealth partners and clinic portals; localized language support (French/Spanish).
   - Tactics: paid search for symptom queries, deep integration bundles with EHR/portal vendors, payer partnerships.
4. Optimization & Partnerships (18+ months)
   - Embed into standard care pathways, explore reimbursement models for triage services, scale to new regions.

Marketing channels & tactics (channel mapped to audience)
- Enterprise sales & BD: targeted outreach to health system VPs (operations, quality), RFP responses, trade shows, peer-reviewed pilot reports.
- Clinical engagement: lunch-and-learns, CME-style workshops, onboarding kits, clinician quick-reference cards.
- Product integrations: developer docs, sandbox API access, clinical rules configuration workshops.
- Patient-facing channels: in-portal banners, symptom page SEO, short explainer videos, mobile push notifications.
- PR & thought leadership: safety-first positioning, publish pilot outcomes, place op-eds on ED overcrowding and triage.
- Paid digital: intent-based search ads (symptoms), programmatic for caregiver audiences, retargeting for pilot regions.
- Community outreach: partnerships with urgent care networks, after-hours clinics, and virtual urgent care providers.

Content & UX playbook (high level)
- Short guided symptom flows: <5 minutes, clear decision points, inline explainability.
- Recommendation cards: clear next step (self-care, primary care, urgent care, ED), rationale, safety cues.
- Escalation prompts: prominent emergency escalation CTA with confident empathy.
- Clinician handoff summaries: structured, time-stamped summary for clinician review.
- Privacy & disclaimer microcopy: concise statements at entry and pre-action screens.

Measurement & analytics
- Implementation telemetry: triage starts/completions, time-to-complete, latency metrics.
- Outcomes linkage: match triage recommendations to claims/EHR outcomes (where allowed) to measure ED diversion and care utilization.
- Safety monitoring: false-negative/under-triage audits, clinician override rates, escalation appropriateness rate.
- Regular reporting cadence: weekly operational dashboards during pilots; monthly summary for stakeholders.

Sales & pricing assumptions (state assumptions explicitly)
- Assumptions:
  - Pricing model: enterprise annual contract + per-active-user usage fee (assumed but to be validated).
  - Sales cycle: 6–9 months for health systems (pilot > contract).
  - Integration effort: 2–6 weeks for basic portal SDK; 8–16 weeks for deep EHR integration.
  - Marketing budget: Not provided — assume a moderate pilot-focused budget (allow $50–150k for pilot marketing and co-development).
  - Clinical validation: Additional regulatory approvals may be required for specific integrations; timeline contingent on partner IRB/reviewer cycles.

Risks & mitigation
- Risk: Perceived automated diagnosis/mistrust. Mitigation: explicit non-diagnostic language, clinician-facing explainability, clinician champions.
- Risk: Regulatory pushback. Mitigation: privacy-first architecture, legal/compliance packaging, early engagement with compliance teams.
- Risk: Poor pilot metrics. Mitigation: strict pilot inclusion criteria, clinician training, short iterative cycles and A/B testing of flows.

Assumptions (explicit)
- Primary markets: US and Canada (English first; French and Spanish support planned later).
- Resource assumptions: marketing budget, sales headcount, and clinical affairs staffing not fully specified — above pricing and budget assumptions are used to guide tactics.
- Data access: we assume access to operational outcomes (EHR/claims) is possible in pilots for measurement; if not, plan for proxy metrics (self-reported follow-up, telehealth visit linkage).
- Legal/regulatory: product will remain non-diagnostic; no current regulatory clearance beyond compliance (MVP stage). Any broadened claims or clinical decision support changes will require legal review.

Deliverables & timeline (high level)
- 0–3 months: 2 pilot launches, clinician training kits, pilot dashboard, initial PR announcement.
- 3–9 months: 3–5 enterprise deals/pilots, publicized case study(s), full integration playbooks, SEM and targeted paid campaigns.
- 9–18 months: language localization, scaled consumer mobile/web deployments, payer outreach.

Budget/Resource priorities (assumed)
- Top priority spend: enterprise sales + clinical validation (40%).
- Engineering for integrations and latency/security (30%).
- Marketing and content (25%).
- Contingency/regulatory/legal (5%).

# 2) Platform-Specific Playbooks

Health System Integrations (EHR/Portal)
- Key message: "Reduce avoidable ED visits with an explainable triage layer that augments care navigation while preserving clinician oversight."
- Primary UX elements: portal-embedded symptom flows; clinician handoff summary cards in EHR inbox; co-branded outcome dashboards.
- Content formats: in-portal symptom entry, short explainer microcopy, downloadable clinician summary PDFs.
- Integration checklist:
  - SSO/SAML/OAuth for user authentication; support for SMART on FHIR where possible.
  - Secure PHI transport; HIPAA-compliant BAA and logging.
  - Low-latency API endpoints; offline fallback for intermittent connectivity.
  - Configurable clinical rules and escalation thresholds per site.
- GTM tactics:
  - Executive briefing pack + pilot proposal template.
  - On-site clinician training and simulation sessions.
  - Pilot success scorecards shared weekly.
- KPIs:
  - Pilot: % reduction in non-urgent ED visits (vs baseline), triage completion rate, clinician handoff open rate.
  - Scaling: integration time, cost per active user.
- Messaging snippets:
  - Clinician: "This triage summary is supplemental — please review and confirm next steps."
  - Patient: "If you are experiencing an emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department."

Clinic Portals & Front-Desk
- Key message: "Streamline triage at check-in and reduce inappropriate ED referrals with fast, explainable guidance."
- Primary UX elements: kiosk/portal pre-check-in flows, front-desk dashboards highlighting suggested disposition.
- Content formats: one-click recommendation cards, printable patient handouts with rationale and follow-up instructions.
- Integration checklist:
  - Simple iframe/SDK embed; minimum latency for in-clinic use.
  - Data minimization: capture only required data fields; consent checkpoint.
  - Local escalation workflows (after-hours routing to on-call).
- GTM tactics:
  - Quick-start embedding guide, plug-and-play script for front-desk workflows.
  - Two-page clinician and staff cheat-sheet.
- KPIs:
  - % of patients routed to same-day primary care instead of ED, front-desk time savings, staff satisfaction.

Telehealth Partners & Clinicians
- Key message: "Provide safer, explainable triage before clinician encounters to prioritize urgent cases and simplify handoffs."
- Primary UX elements: pre-visit symptom triage, clinician-facing summary integrated into telehealth session.
- Content formats: structured clinician handoff, optional audio summary, in-session recommendation overlay.
- Integration checklist:
  - Real-time handoff via API or embedded widget.
  - Support for clinician override and feedback logging.
  - Audit trail for safety monitoring.
- GTM tactics:
  - Integrate with telehealth scheduling to require triage for same-day evaluations.
  - Host clinician webinars demonstrating improved case mix and time-saving.
- KPIs:
  - Reduction in low-acuity telehealth-to-ED escalations, clinician time saved per session, override rate.

Consumer Web & Mobile Apps
- Key message: "Get immediate, clear, and private guidance for your symptoms — with emergency escalation if needed."
- Primary UX elements: concise guided symptom flows (3–7 steps), actionable recommendation cards, clear emergency CTA.
- Content formats: short explainer videos, FAQ, privacy badges, shareable after-action summaries for clinician visits.
- Integration checklist:
  - Mobile-first responsive design; <5s flow completion.
  - Privacy-first consent flows, transparent data use statements.
  - Localization for French/Spanish (planned) and culturally sensitive microcopy.
- GTM tactics:
  - SEO strategy targeting urgent symptom queries and "should I go to ER" queries.
  - App store assets emphasizing safety, explainability, and privacy.
  - Partnerships with urgent care chains to offer co-branded experiences.
- KPIs:
  - Triage completion rate, recommendation acceptance rate, app retention at 7/30 days, referral lift to recommended care types.

Regulatory & Compliance Playbook (cross-platform)
- Mandatory items:
  - HIPAA (US) and PIPEDA (Canada) compliance; BAAs in place for all enterprise partners.
  - Clear non-diagnostic disclaimers inline and in marketing materials.
  - Emergency escalation protocol prominent and unavoidable for red-flag conditions.
  - Logging and audit trails for every recommendation and escalation.
- Clinical governance:
  - Clinical advisory board oversight for rules and AI model behavior.
  - Post-deployment safety audits and reportable incident workflow.
- Messaging/legal:
  - All public-facing content includes a safety-first disclaimer and contact for clinical questions.

Localization & Cultural Sensitivity
- Base launch in English (US/Canada). Plan phased rollout:
  - Phase 1: English (core).
  - Phase 2: French (Canada) and Spanish (US), with adapted microcopy and clinician handoff translations.
- Cultural notes: avoid alarmist phrasing; prefer calm, action-oriented language. Localize examples (clinic names, phone numbers) and emergency contacts.

Clinician Engagement & Adoption Tactics
- Identify clinician champions early and co-create flows.
- Provide one-click feedback inside handoff summaries.
- Quarterly clinician roundtables to iterate content and thresholds.
- Offer dashboards that show reduced low-acuity ED arrivals attributable to triage.

Creative & Copy Guidelines (microcopy examples)
- Onboarding: "This tool helps guide you to the right care. It is not a diagnosis. If you feel your life is at risk, call emergency services now."
- Recommendation card header: "Based on your answers, we recommend: Primary care today"
- Escalation prompt: "This looks potentially serious — please call 911 or go to the emergency department now."
